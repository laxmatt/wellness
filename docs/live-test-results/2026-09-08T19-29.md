### The score overstates how well this worked

8 of 15 passed, but the seven failures share one reply, verbatim:

> I could not read that reliably. Could you say it another way?

That is not the model's wording. It is the fixed fallback in
`OpenAIProvider.exchange`, returned when `ModelIntent.safeParse` rejects the
model's JSON. So on those seven, the model answered, was charged for, and its
entire output was discarded before it reached the shopper.

Every case that expected constraints and failed produced that fallback. The
cases that passed are the ones where the correct answer was **no constraints at
all**: two factual questions, a superlative, a vague opener, a medical refusal
handled by the route, and an unmappable preference. One case, "under 500",
passed with a real extracted constraint, so the schema does accept some outputs.

Read that way, the honest reading of extraction is: of the eight cases where a
careful person would have entered constraints, **one** produced them. Seven were
dropped whole.

### What is confirmed, and what is not

**Confirmed.** The seven failing replies are the `ModelIntent` parse-failure
fallback. That string appears in exactly one place in the codebase, and it is
reached only when `safeParse` fails.

**Not established.** Which field of those payloads violated the schema. The
rejected JSON is not retained anywhere: `safeParse` fails, the fallback is
substituted, and the payload is dropped. Candidates worth checking, none of them
confirmed by anything observed here: an `op` outside the ten-value enum in
`Condition`, a `direction` outside the three-value enum in `SoftPreference`
(`normalize` supplies a default only when the field is absent, not when it is
present and wrong), or a `weight` outside 0 to 1. Deciding between them needs the
rejected payload, which means retaining it on parse failure and running again.

### Were the answers useful and grounded

Mixed, and the split is clean.

**Grounded, where a reply survived.** The model quoted only sourced figures and
named its limits rather than filling gaps. It cited 189 mW/cm² for a specific
product on the irradiance question, listed 36 °F and 37 °F for named tubs and
said plainly that the Ice Barrel models have no stated lowest temperature, and
answered the 660nm versus 850nm question by saying the catalogue does not state
it rather than reaching for general knowledge. It declined "which one is
healthiest" on its own, and asked for a budget instead of guessing at "I have no
idea where to start". No invented constraints appeared in any case, including
the seven that failed.

**Not useful, on the cases that matter most.** Seven of the eight requests that
carried real shopping constraints ended in an error sentence. A shopper saying
"zero sugar electrolytes under $2 a serving" is the core case for this feature,
and it got nothing.

One reply is worth a second look even though it passed: "under 500" returned
"There are no products listed under $500 in the catalogue." The red-light
category contains products under $500. The claim is the model's own, in prose,
and the site's engine, not the model, decides what matches. Whether the shortlist
it was shown contained no such product, or the model misread what it was shown,
is not established here.
