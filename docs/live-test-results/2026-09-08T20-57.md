### The one rejected output, and exactly why

Diagnostics captured it. One reply of fifteen was rejected, `finish_reason:
stop`, so it was a complete answer that broke the contract rather than a
truncated one.

```
issue: hard.1.op | invalid_value |
  Invalid option: expected one of "lt"|"lte"|"gt"|"gte"|"eq"|"neq"|"in"|"includes"|"exists"|"missing"
```

The offending constraint, from the captured payload:

```json
{"key":"chiller_included","op":"true","value":true}
```

The model put the value in the operator field. This is the first time the
rejected field has been known rather than guessed at, which is what the
diagnostics were built for. Note what it is not: the enumerated contract added
in `3abcf3d` was followed for the other two constraints in the same payload
(`price` used `lte`, `placement` used `includes`), and fourteen of fifteen
replies validated. One boolean constraint went wrong.

### The most serious finding is not in the score: price units

Three cases show the model sending a price in **dollars** where the filter is in
**integer cents**, which the vocabulary line states.

| Shopper said | Model sent | Engine read it as | Engine matched | Should have matched |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| under $700 | `price lte 700` | $7.00 | 0 | 1 |
| under 500 | `price lte 500` | $5.00 | 0 | 1 |
| up to $5,000 | `price lte 5000` | $50.00 | (reply rejected) | n/a |

This is worse than a failed extraction. The engine faithfully applied the
constraint it was given, the summary faithfully reported the engine, and the
shopper was told that nothing matches their budget when something does. Every
layer behaved correctly on a wrong input, so nothing flagged it.

The strengthened live test is what caught it. The previous version compared
filter keys only, and `price lte 700` contains the key `price`, so it would have
scored both of these as passes.

### Wording review, beyond what the script checks

**Answering from general knowledge instead of the catalogue.** Asked about 660nm
versus 850nm, the reply describes "surface-level skin benefits", light that
"penetrates deeper into tissues", and "different therapeutic effects". None of
that is in the catalogue, and rule 1 requires saying so instead. It also drifts
towards therapeutic claims on a site that must not make them. The same case in
the run of 19:29 answered correctly, that the catalogue does not state it, so
this boundary is not held reliably by instruction.

**Quoting a placeholder price as fact.** "the Hooga HG300 is a compact device
with a targeted coverage area, priced at $139". That price is a placeholder:
`priceIsPlaceholder` is true, and the catalogue block marks it
`[placeholder price, do not rely on it]`. The model relied on it and told a
shopper a number that is demo data.

**Dropping attribution.** "It has an irradiance of 73 mW/cm²" is
`manufacturer_reported` and is stated as fact. In the caffeine reply, the 0 mg
figures for Cure Hydration and Liquid I.V. carry provenance recorded as
`unknown`, so they reach the model as `[unattributed]`, and the reply presents
them as measured values. Rule 2 covers both cases and neither was followed.

**Leaking internal markers into prose.** The temperature reply printed
"36 °F [manufacturer_claim]" verbatim. The attribution is right and the form is
not: those brackets are the internal evidence tier, not English a shopper should
read.

**What went right, and is worth keeping.** The shortlist hedging works: replies
now say "the shortlist does not have any products that fit" rather than claiming
the catalogue is empty, which is the failure of the 19:29 run. The medical
boundary held on the tendonitis question and, separately, "which one is
healthiest" was answered rather than refused, so the substring fix behaves in
production as it does in the tests. The unreadable reply changed nothing: no
proposals, no cleared filters.

### What this run says about readiness

Not ready. The price unit defect alone makes the assistant tell shoppers the
opposite of the truth about their budget, which is the most common constraint on
the site. The placeholder price quoted as fact is a second, independent
integrity problem. Both are fixable and neither is fixed here: this file records
the run, not a repair.
