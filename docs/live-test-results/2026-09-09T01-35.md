## Strict mode was on, and two replies still died

Appended by hand. The section above is generated by
`src/domain/livetest-report.ts`, which does not know what `response_format`
was sent or why a reply was rejected.

`ASSISTANT_RESPONSE_FORMAT=json_schema` was set on the server process, verified
in `/proc/<pid>/environ` before any request. Every one of the 14 model calls
carried `strict: true`. No request was refused: the schema compiled on all of
them, as the single request at 01:28 established it would.

**Schema-valid is not correctly interpreted, and this run shows both failures
separately.**

Two of the three failures are not interpretation failures at all. Both replies
were schema-valid, both extracted the right thing, and both were discarded by
`ModelIntent` on a bound the schema declines to express:

| Case | What the model actually returned | Why it was discarded |
| --- | --- | --- |
| red-light, "under 500" | `price lte {amount: 500, currency: "USD"}` | `suggestCompare` had 5 ids; the limit is 4 |
| wellness-drinks, "Which one is healthiest?" | `hard: []`, `soft: []`, `medicalIntent: true` | `suggestCompare` had 6 ids; the limit is 4 |

Read from the diagnostics file, not inferred. Both would have passed. "under
500" is the money contract followed exactly: dollars in an object, converted to
50000 cents by code. "Which one is healthiest?" is the case that must receive
the fixed shopping clarification rather than the clinician line, and it would
have: the route decides that from the site's own detector and ignores the
model's flag.

So extraction was right on 14 of 15. The score of 12 counts two replies that
were thrown away after the model got them right.

**This falsifies a claim committed earlier today.** `docs/ASSISTANT.md` says
omitting the size keywords "costs nothing: `ModelIntent` enforces every count
and every range on every reply". The enforcement half is true. The cost half is
not. Enforcement here means discarding the whole reply, and it cost two of
fifteen cases. `suggestCompare` is capped at 4 in Zod and unbounded in the
schema, so strict mode generates a 5- or 6-element array, the provider accepts
it, and the shopper gets "I could not read that reliably."

Left open deliberately: this run was authorized to assess extraction, not to
change anything. The correction and whatever follows from it are backlog.

**The one real interpretation failure** is wellness-drinks, "Zero sugar
electrolytes under $2 a serving". The reply was valid, readable, and wrong in
three ways:

- `sugar_g` was dropped entirely. "Zero sugar" is the first two words of the
  sentence and it is a filter key.
- `price_per_serving_minor lte 200` admits exactly $2.00. "Under $2" admits at
  most $1.99. The operator and the amount are judged together, so `lte 200` and
  `lt 200` are different requests and only one of them is what was asked.
- No `function` preference, though "electrolytes" is one.

The shopper is then told "3 of the 6 products in this category match", which is
true of the constraint that was applied and not of the one they asked for. No
schema can catch this. The JSON was perfect.

**A composition defect, visible in a passing case.** cold-plunge, "I don't want
to deal with an electrician" scored ok, because the `plumbing` preference is
what the case checks for. The sentence it produced is not usable English:

> I have read that as none. fill with a hose. power and plumbing. 3 of the 6
> products in this category match.

`reply-composer.ts` is rendering enum labels straight into a sentence. Backlog,
not this run.
