### Two extraction defects, both new

**C4 invented a budget out of the catalogue.** "The cheapest one that still
has a chiller" came back as `chiller_included eq true` **and** `price lte
549000`, which is to the cent the price of the cheapest chiller tub. The reply
reads "chiller included, price of $5,490 or less. 1 of the 6 products in this
category matches." A superlative became a hard budget, and the two tubs the
shopper might have traded up to were excluded from a search they never
narrowed.

The constraint check passed this: `checkReply` accepts a hard constraint where
a preference was expected, so `price` being present at all satisfied it. The
product assertion is what caught it, and this is the case for keeping product
assertions rather than scoring constraints alone.

**D4 misread a decimal budget by a factor of a hundred.** "Something under
$1.60 a serving" came back as `price_per_serving_minor lt 16000`, and the
reply says "price per serving under $160". The money contract was followed in
form, an object in dollars rather than a bare number, and the amount inside it
was wrong. Three products matched a limit a hundred times too generous.

The contract's own examples show 700 and 1.99. This is not the unit confusion
the contract was written for; it is the amount itself.

With the two earlier samples of the same sentence, `sugar_g` has now been

**Five samples cannot separate systematic failure from variance, and this run
does not claim to.** What they support is a count, and the count is what is
written above. `function` at 0 of 5 is the stronger signal of the two and the
better candidate for investigation; `sugar_g` at 2 of 5 says only that the
sentence is unreliable.

### The missing-value safeguard, live for the first time

It fired four times: on C1 and on all three repetitions, each time saying what
had not been filtered and asking about it with the catalogue's own values.
Three of those were `function`, exactly the omission above, so a shopper would
have been told and offered the fix rather than left with a silently narrower
search.

**It also produced a false positive.** C1's "A tub with a chiller" names
`tub`, which is a value of `tub_type` as well as the ordinary noun for the
product, so the site asked "Which tub type suits you?" when nothing had been
missed. A whole-word match cannot tell a category noun from a filter value.
Recorded, not fixed.
