## Which product this is

The page is explicitly Next Gen Original. Citrus and Berry are separate
choices. The record is scoped to Original before any figure was merged, in the
name and in every note, the same way the OLIPOP record was scoped to the
refrigerated formula and the BIOMAX record to its ninth generation. Three
readings in a row have needed this, which is a pattern rather than a
coincidence: a product page is usually one variant of several.

## What the page stated

30 servings, one 13 g level scoop. 40 calories, 2 g of fiber, strictly under
1 g of total sugar, 35 mg sodium, 250 mg potassium. Stevia leaf extract.
Contains soy. $99 one-time plus $9 shipping, with a 30-day subscription at $79
and a 90-day subscription at $207, and a first-order promotional price.

## What was recorded

- **$99 one-time**, read. Shipping is recorded as `$9 shipping, stated on the
  product page` rather than folded into the amount: a shopper comparing pack
  prices is not comparing order totals.
- **The first-order promotion is not recorded at all.** It is what a new
  customer pays once, not what this pack costs, and putting it on a comparison
  page would compare a promotion against other products' ordinary prices.
- **Serving size "1 level scoop (13 g)"**, read, replacing a placeholder
  "about 12 g" that this repository had built out of a relayed "12 to 13 g"
  range. The placeholder also said "in 8 oz water"; the reading did not cover a
  mixing volume, so that half is not carried forward as though it had been.
- **Calories 40**, read, replacing a relayed 50 the page does not support.
- **Sodium 35 mg and potassium 250 mg**, read, two fields this record did not
  have.
- **Fiber 2 g and total sugar under 1 g**, confirmed, with the sugar still
  recorded as a bound because the bound is the only number the label states.
- **Stevia leaf extract**, confirmed. The page also says the product contains
  soy. That is an allergen, not a sweetener, and this catalogue has no allergen
  field, so it went into the description rather than being forced into the
  sweetener list. Inventing a field for one product is worse than a sentence a
  reader can see.
- **Subscription available**, read, now naming both cadences.

## What was withdrawn

**The subscription discount percentage.** The record said 20 per cent,
computed here from a relayed $99 to $79. The page offers two subscriptions at
different cadences: $79 for 30 days is about 20 per cent off the one-time pack,
and $207 for 90 days works out nearer 30 per cent for the same quantity. Two
rates do not imply one number, and picking either states a discount the page
does not. The field is `not_stated`, both prices are in the note, and the card
now says "Subscription available" instead of naming a figure.

## What was not recorded

**Added sugar.** The record carries a relayed 0 g. That figure was not in the
table this reading retrieved, so it stays exactly as it was, still cited to the
2026-09-08 search summary and still marked relayed. It was not promoted, and it
was not withdrawn either: a reading that did not reach a figure says nothing
about whether the label states it.

**Caffeine.** Still `not_stated`, for the same reason it always was: the brand
says no added caffeine and notes trace caffeine from green tea extract with no
amount. The reading did not show a figure either. Absence of a number is not a
zero.

**Dietary claims.** Vegan, gluten free and non-GMO remain prototype data. They
were not part of this reading.

**Stock, images, health claims.** None verified.
